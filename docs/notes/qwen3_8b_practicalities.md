# Practical state (Nov 2026) of training and serving an EAGLE-style / MTP-style draft head for Qwen3-8B

Research date: 2026-11-11. "Verified" below means the page was opened during this research and the fact was read from it (via a page-summarising fetch tool, so numbers in image-only charts could not be read). Anything taken only from a search snippet is marked "(snippet only)".

## Existing draft heads for Qwen3 models

### Takeaway
Qwen3-8B is one of the best-covered bases for EAGLE-3: at least five independent public EAGLE-3 heads exist (RedHatAI, AngelSlim/Tencent, Tengyunw, WhiFlash, SwitchXDDD), plus non-EAGLE drafters (DFlash, DSpark, DFly, UNO LoRA) and an AngelSlim MTP-style drafter result. No one appears to have published a Qwen3.5-style *native MTP module* retrofitted onto Qwen3-8B that a stock server loads as `mtp`; the published heads are EAGLE-3-format (one decoder layer, ~0.4B params with a 32K draft vocab), all trained with thinking disabled where stated.

### Cited Findings

**WhiFlash/Qwen3-8B-EAGLE3** (targets SGLang; the most fully documented recipe)
- 0.4B params, `LlamaForCausalLMEagle3`, 1 decoder layer, hidden 4096, 32 heads / 8 KV heads, fixed 32,000-token draft vocabulary (target vocab 151,936). — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/WhiFlash/Qwen3-8B-EAGLE3)

### Inferences
- The ~0.4B head size is consistent with one Llama-style layer at d=4096 (attention about 67M, MLP about 151M if intermediate size is 12288, fusion FC 3x4096->4096 about 50M) plus a 32,000 x 4096 draft LM head (about 131M). The RedHatAI file's 1.02B is most likely the same head plus a stored copy of the 151,936 x 4096 embedding table (about 622M); the BOOL 151,936 and I64 32,000 tensors are the vocab-mapping buffers. This breakdown is my arithmetic, not stated on any card.
- Acceptance-length figures across cards are not comparable: RedHat/AngelSlim use chain drafting with k=2-7 in vLLM (about 2.0-2.8), WhiFlash uses a 60-node tree in SGLang (4.78). The reader's planned head should be compared at matched settings.
- AngelSlim's "MTP" row on Qwen3-8B (3.24) is the closest public evidence that an MTP-style drafter has been trained for Qwen3-8B, but I did not find a released Qwen3-8B MTP checkpoint (the README names MTP/DFly weights only for Hy3-A21B).

### Gaps
- I did not open the pages for RedHatAI/Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B speculators, the Zjcxy-SmartAI repos, `alexchen4ai/qwen3-8B-eagle`, `amd/PARD2-Qwen3-8B`, or any `nm-testing` repos; they appeared only in search results or the EAGLE README list. No `yuhuili` Qwen3 checkpoint was found (EAGLE README says none is official).
- The `AngelSlim/angelspec` HF collection was not opened, so whether a Qwen3-8B MTP/DFly checkpoint is downloadable is unverified.
- RedHatAI's speedup-vs-concurrency charts for Qwen3-8B are images; numeric values were not recoverable.

## Training toolkits

### Takeaway
Three maintained toolkits ship a ready Qwen3-8B EAGLE-3 recipe: `speculators` (vLLM project; online and offline, and the only one with a first-class MTP trainer, but that trainer finetunes an existing native MTP head on Qwen3.5), SpecForge (SGLang; online and offline), and Tencent AngelSpec (online only, includes MTP + TTT). The convergent recipe is: regenerate responses with the target model, thinking off, 32K draft vocab, TTT length 7, and from ~0.5M to 1.4M conversations for production quality; none of the toolkits documents guidance for training on Qwen3 thinking traces.

### Cited Findings

**speculators (vllm-project/speculators, formerly neuralmagic)**
- Trainable algorithms: Eagle-3, P-EAGLE (deprecated, removal scheduled for 0.11), DFlash, DFlash2, DSpark, MTP; each in online or offline mode. Requires Python 3.11+ and `vllm>=0.27.1` in a separate venv. — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- Pipeline commands: `speculators prepare-data --model Qwen/Qwen3-8B --data ./target_responses.jsonl --output ./output --max-samples 5000 --seq-length 8192 --no-skip-token-freq`; `python scripts/launch_vllm.py Qwen/Qwen3-8B [-- --data-parallel-size 2 --port 8000]`; `speculators generate-offline-data --preprocessed-data ./output --endpoint http://localhost:8000/v1 --output ./output/hidden_states --concurrency 32 --validate-outputs` (offline only; one `hs_N.safetensors` per sample; resumable; `--world-size/--rank` to shard across machines); `speculators train` (or `torchrun --standalone --nproc_per_node N -m speculators.train`) with `--draft-vocab-size 32000`, `--epochs` (default 20; examples use 5), `--total-seq-len 8192`. — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- Online mode: `--vllm-endpoint http://localhost:8000/v1 --on-missing generate`, hidden states generated on demand and discarded; example splits 2 GPUs for vLLM + 2 for training. Offline: `--hidden-states-path ... --on-missing raise`, all 4 GPUs for training. — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- Storage formula for offline mode: `avg_seq_len x num_layers x hidden_size x dtype_bytes`. Example for Llama-3.1-8B (8192 tokens, 4 layers, hidden 4096, 2-byte dtype): about 268 MB per sample; 1K samples about 260 GB, 10K about 2.6 TB, 50K about 13 TB. — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- An earlier version of the tutorial quoted about 1.6 TB for 50K samples at average sequence length 1024 (snippet only; consistent with the formula). — [search result for older offline tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/stable/user_guide/tutorials/train_eagle3_offline/)
- Ready scripts: `eagle3_qwen3_8b_ultrachat_online_5k.sh`, `eagle3_llama3_8b_ultrachat_offline_5k.sh`, `dflash_qwen3_8b_ultrachat_online_5k.sh`, `dflash2_qwen3_8b_ultrachat_online_5k.sh`, `dspark_qwen3_0_6b_sharegpt_online.sh`, `mtp_qwen3_5_9b_gsm8k_online.sh`. The documented examples are 5K-sample sanity runs; the tutorial gives no training wall-clock, no Eagle-3 learning rate, and no TTT-step setting. `prepare-data` takes 15 s to 2 min for 5K samples. — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- MTP in speculators: "MTP finetunes the verifier's own multi-token-prediction head instead of training a draft from scratch"; it "requires a verifier with native MTP layers" (listed: Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.5-0.8B). Capture only the final layer (`--target-layer-ids 32` for the 32-layer Qwen3.5-9B), train 3 epochs, then `speculators stitch-mtp ./output/checkpoints/checkpoint_best Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B --output-path ./output/stitched` and serve with `vllm serve ./output/stitched --speculative-config '{"method":"mtp","num_speculative_tokens":3}' --no-enable-chunked-prefill`. — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- Sanity-run quality at 5K samples is poor: DFlash on 5K ShareGPT, 5 epochs gives acceptance length 1.47; P-EAGLE 1.53; "most methods reach roughly 40% first-token acceptance on similar data". — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- Response regeneration: `./scripts/response_regeneration/run_all.sh --model <model> --dataset magpie --limit 1000` (datasets `magpie`, `hermes-fc`, `ultrachat`; `--dp-size`, `--tp-size`, `--reasoning-parser qwen3`, `--tool-call-parser hermes`); "about 10 minutes on 2x H100 GPUs for 1K samples". Recommended before training because data become on-policy. The page does not mention `enable_thinking` or whether reasoning content is kept. — [Response Regeneration tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/response_regeneration/)
- Other tutorials: multi-node training with Mooncake streaming, RAG training data, evaluating performance, throughput vs interactivity, serve in vLLM. — [speculators tutorials index](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/)
- EAGLE-3 head in speculators: selected target-layer hidden states concatenated, FC, Llama-style decoder layers (default 1), LM head; trained with KL divergence to target logits; supports reduced/cross-tokenizer vocab. — [speculators Eagle-3 page](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/algorithms/eagle3/)

**SpecForge (sgl-project/SpecForge)**
- Latest: v0.3.0, released with SpecBundle phase 2 in 2026-08. Methods: EAGLE3, EAGLE3.1, P-EAGLE, DFlash, DFlash2, Domino, DSpark. MTP is not listed. EAGLE3 supports online (external) and offline (colocated and disaggregated) modes. — [SpecForge README](https://github.com/sgl-project/SpecForge)
- Qwen3-8B configs in `examples/configs/`: `online/disaggregated/external/qwen3-8b-eagle3.yaml`, `offline/colocated/qwen3-8b-eagle3.yaml`, `offline/disaggregated/qwen3-8b-eagle3.yaml`, plus `qwen3-8b-peagle.yaml`, `qwen3-8b-dflash.yaml`, `qwen3-8b-domino.yaml` variants. — [SpecForge README](https://github.com/sgl-project/SpecForge)
- The offline colocated Qwen3-8B config: target served by the sglang backend in bfloat16, draft config `configs/qwen3-8b-eagle3.json`, hidden-state cache `./cache/hidden_states/qwen3-8b-sharegpt`, max length 4096, qwen chat template, 10 epochs, batch size 1, lr 1e-4, grad clip 0.5, `ttt_length` 7, flex_attention. — [qwen3-8b-eagle3.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sgl-project/SpecForge/main/examples/configs/offline/colocated/qwen3-8b-eagle3.yaml)
- SpecBundle production recipe: Open-PerfectBlend, 1.4M conversations (chat, math, coding, instruction following), responses regenerated by the target at temperature 0.8, trained from scratch 2 epochs, lr 1e-4, cosine schedule, seq length 4096. — [SpecForge paper, arXiv 2603.18567](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- Data scale matters: ShareGPT (120K conversations) + UltraChat (200K) gave acceptance length 2.82 on Math500 versus 3.48 with Perfect-Blend (1.4M), about 1.17x more speedup. Regenerating data with the target (Llama-3.1-8B) raised throughput 5.3% on average. — [SpecForge paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- TTT length: default 7; tested 1-17; MT-Bench peaks around 3 while Math500/GSM8K/HumanEval peak around 13; 3 or 5 for low-memory setups. — [SpecForge paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- Reasoning: "The current release focuses on instruct models. Reasoning and vision-language models are planned for future releases." — [SpecForge paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)

**SafeAILab/EAGLE (official)**
- EAGLE-3 training entry point: `cd eagle/traineagle3; deepspeed main.py --deepspeed_config ds_config.json`; README itself recommends SpecForge for out-of-the-box EAGLE-3 training with SGLang. README includes a Qwen3 evaluation script `gen_ea_answer_qwen3` with `--use_eagle3`. For Qwen2 targets it advises bf16 instead of fp16 to avoid overflow, and notes English-only training data underperforms on Chinese. — [SafeAILab/EAGLE README](https://github.com/SafeAILab/EAGLE)
- EAGLE-3 paper recipe: ShareGPT (~68K entries) + UltraChat-200K (~464K entries), target-generated responses; AdamW betas (0.9, 0.95), grad clip 0.5, lr 5e-5; for the reasoning model DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA 8B they added OpenThoughts-114k-math. Training cost, time and GPU count are not reported in the paper. — [EAGLE-3 paper, arXiv 2503.01840](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840)

**Tencent AngelSlim / AngelSpec**
- AngelSpec (git submodule `third_party/AngelSpec/`, released 26/07/29) trains DFly, DFlare, DFlash, DSpark, MTP, Eagle3; "MTP + TTT" and DFly are the lead methods. Online-only disaggregated design: inference engines (vLLM first-class, also SGLang, HF) extract multi-layer hidden states, streamed via Mooncake over RDMA to FSDP2 training workers "with no disk staging"; includes vocabulary pruning of the draft lm_head and online acceptance-rate evaluation. Quickstart: `./examples/qwen3-8b-single-node/run.sh`, 4 GPUs (2 inference, 2 training). Eagle3 training/deployment arrived in AngelSlim v0.3 (26/01/13). Datasets and training time are not stated. — [Tencent/AngelSlim README](https://github.com/Tencent/AngelSlim)

**Others**
- NVIDIA NeMo AutoModel ships EAGLE-3 precompute/cache components; its docs put the offline cache at "on the order of 100+ MB per sample for an 8B target at seq-len 2048, i.e. multiple TB for a large corpus" and say offline caching "has largely been superseded by online training" (snippet only). — [NeMo AutoModel eagle3_cache docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/automodel/nemo-automodel/nemo_automodel/components/datasets/llm/eagle3_cache)

### Inferences
- For "one transformer layer reading frozen last hidden states plus token embeddings", no toolkit matches exactly out of the box: EAGLE-3 trainers fuse three layers (low/mid/high) and expect an EAGLE-3 checkpoint format; the speculators MTP trainer and `stitch-mtp` assume the verifier already has native MTP layers (Qwen3.5). The lowest-friction route with existing tooling is to train an EAGLE-3 head (well-supported for Qwen3-8B everywhere); a last-layer-only MTP-style head would need either a custom architecture in AngelSpec/speculators or custom server code.
- Thinking mode: every Qwen3-8B head that states its setting was trained and evaluated with thinking disabled (RedHatAI card, AngelSpec benchmarks), and SpecForge explicitly defers reasoning models. The only published precedent for reasoning traces is the EAGLE-3 paper adding OpenThoughts-114k-math for DeepSeek-R1-Distill. A reader who serves Qwen3-8B with thinking on should regenerate training responses with thinking on (speculators' regeneration script accepts `--reasoning-parser qwen3`), but there is no measured guidance on the mix.

### Gaps
- No toolkit documents wall-clock training time or GPU-hours for the Qwen3-8B EAGLE-3 recipe at production data scale.
- The contents of SpecForge's `configs/qwen3-8b-eagle3.json` (draft vocab size, layer ids) and the online Qwen3-8B YAML were not opened. SpecForge's own offline storage figure was not found on its README.
- Default `--target-layer-ids` in speculators' `launch_vllm.py` are not listed in the tutorial.
- I found no documented guidance in any toolkit on Qwen3 `enable_thinking` during data generation.

## Serving support

### Takeaway
Both vLLM and SGLang serve EAGLE-3 heads on a Qwen3-8B base today with stock flags, and public checkpoints for exactly that pairing exist for each server. `mtp` in both servers is tied to model families that ship native MTP weights (vLLM has per-architecture MTP types such as `qwen3_5_mtp` and `qwen3_next_mtp`, none for plain `qwen3`), so a Qwen3.5-style MTP module bolted onto Qwen3-8B is not documented as loadable without code; vLLM's experimental custom-speculator plugin hook is the sanctioned route for that.

### Cited Findings

**vLLM**

A "Custom Speculators (Experimental)" section shows `"method": "my_spec", "model": "<draft-model>", "speculator_options": {...}` registered through a vLLM plugin. vLLM also has a "Hidden State Extraction" method (`extract_hidden_states`) used for training-data generation. — [vLLM speculative decoding docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/usage/speculative_decoding/)
- Ascend-plugin docs state EAGLE drafts should run with draft tensor parallelism 1 while the main model may use TP (plugin docs, not upstream; snippet only). — [vLLM Ascend speculative decoding guide](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/ascend/en/latest/user_guide/feature_guide/speculative_decoding.html)

- `--speculative-algorithm` values: `EAGLE` (alias `NEXTN`; EAGLE-2 style feature drafting with tree verification), `EAGLE3` (recommended), `MTP` (run through the EAGLE workflow), `UNO`, `DFLASH`, `STANDALONE`, `NGRAM`. — [SGLang speculative decoding docs](https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/speculative_decoding.html)

### Inferences
- A Qwen3.5-style MTP module for a Qwen3-8B base cannot be assumed to load in stock vLLM: the `mtp` resolution is keyed on the target's model type and there is a `qwen3_5_mtp` / `qwen3_next_mtp` type but no plain-`qwen3` MTP type in the literal list. The Gemma 4 precedent shows vLLM can load a separate MTP checkpoint via `"model"`, but only through a family-specific model class. Realistic options: (a) train in EAGLE-3 format instead (stock support in both servers); (b) write a vLLM custom speculator plugin (`register_speculative_method`, experimental); (c) patch an MTP class for Qwen3. I could not see the `qwen3_5_mtp` mapping code (page truncated), so whether it could be coerced via config override is unverified.
- SGLang's `MTP` similarly expects the MTP weights to follow a known family layout; EAGLE3 with a `LlamaForCausalLMEagle3`-format checkpoint is the stock path for Qwen3-8B.
- Lossless guarantee caveat for the reader: both servers verify against the target distribution, but vLLM states this holds only up to numerical precision, and optional modes (FLy in vLLM; acceptance thresholds below 1.0 in SGLang) are lossy and must be left off.

### Gaps
- No documentation found on EAGLE-3 with quantized (AWQ/GPTQ/FP8) Qwen3-8B targets in either server; vLLM docs do not mention quantized targets, SGLang only offers a draft-quantization flag.
- TGI: no evidence of EAGLE/MTP head support was found (not listed in the EAGLE README integrations; no page opened). TensorRT-LLM support is asserted by the EAGLE README only.
- llama.cpp merge status of EAGLE3/MTP was not confirmed on github.com directly; whether its MTP path accepts a retrofitted head for a base without native MTP is unknown.
- The vLLM version of the "latest" docs was not stated on the pages (speculators tutorial requires `vllm>=0.27.1`; benchmark pages cite 0.29.0 and 0.30.1rc1).

## Measured production results

### Takeaway
On Qwen3-8B, measured single-stream speedups are about 1.7x in vLLM with a 2-token chain (accept length ~2.0) and about 2.0-2.4x in SGLang with deeper/tree drafting (accept length up to 4.78); chain acceptance length saturates near 2.3-2.8 for k >= 4. The best quantified evidence on load is the EAGLE-3 paper's Llama-3.1-8B tables: the gain decays from ~1.8x at batch 2 to ~1.4x at batch 64 in SGLang and to ~1.0x at batch 56 in vLLM; a newer vLLM study on a 27B Qwen shows drafter KV-cache memory, not compute, becoming the limit on concurrency.

### Inferences
- For a single-layer chain-drafting head on Qwen3-8B in vLLM, a realistic expectation from the two independent public heads is acceptance length ~2.0-2.5 at k=2-3 and ~1.7x single-stream; SGLang with tree drafting extracts more per head (2.0-2.4x) at batch 1.
- The native-MTP result on Qwen3.8-27B (2.0-2.1x with only 2 draft tokens, and 4x more concurrent capacity than a heavy drafter) suggests an MTP-style last-layer head is competitive at batch 1 and degrades more gracefully under load than deep drafters, but this is a different, larger model with a co-trained head.
- Under saturation, expect the benefit to shrink toward ~1.0-1.4x; the cause shifts from compute overhead (EAGLE-3 paper) to KV-cache memory per request (speculators study), so concurrency caps must be re-measured with the head loaded.

### Gaps
- No public numeric concurrency sweep for Qwen3-8B + EAGLE-3 in either server was found (RedHatAI's are image charts; a Kaitchup article on Qwen3-32B is paywalled and unread).
- No Qwen3-8B results with thinking enabled were found.
- The speculators study did not report the speedup value at which each drafter falls below 1x.

## Cost estimate inputs

### Takeaway
No source publishes an end-to-end GPU-hour figure for a 7-8B EAGLE-3 head; the usable inputs are (i) SpecForge's measured online training throughput of ~127K tok/s on 8x H200 for Llama-3.1-8B (target forward plus draft update), (ii) dataset sizes of 0.5M-1.4M conversations / ~1B tokens with 2 to ~14 epochs, and (iii) the offline cache cost of 8 KB per token per captured layer at d=4096 in bf16. These imply single-digit to low-tens of H200-hours per billion token-epochs online, and tens of TB if caching offline, which is why all three toolkits now default to or recommend online mode.

### Cited Findings
- SpecForge EAGLE-3 training throughput, 8x H200, seq length 4096: Llama-3.1-8B baseline 63,015 tok/s (1.04 s/step); SpecForge 126,640 tok/s at batch 64 (2.07 s/step), 2.01x. Qwen3-30B-A3B 126,031 tok/s (TP=4). — [SpecForge paper, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- SpecForge kernel work: 62.1% less attention-kernel time and 93.5% less peak attention memory at seq 4096 on one H200; stored attention logits were 80% of activation memory in the naive TTT path; draft model is about 3-5% the size of the target. — [SpecForge paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- Dataset scales used in practice: EAGLE-3 paper ShareGPT ~68K + UltraChat ~464K entries; Tengyunw Qwen3-8B "about 600K samples and 1B tokens"; SpecBundle 1.4M conversations x 2 epochs; WhiFlash 464K examples, 800K steps at batch 8 on 4x H100. — [EAGLE-3 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01840); [Tengyunw card](https://huggingface.co/Tengyunw/qwen3_8b_eagle3); [SpecForge paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567); [WhiFlash card](https://huggingface.co/WhiFlash/Qwen3-8B-EAGLE3)
- Offline hidden-state storage: `avg_seq_len x num_layers x hidden_size x dtype_bytes`; about 268 MB per 8192-token sample for 4 layers at hidden 4096, 2 bytes; 50K such samples about 13 TB. — [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)
- Response regeneration throughput: "about 10 minutes on 2x H100 GPUs for 1K samples" for the full scripted pipeline (includes server start/stop). — [Response Regeneration tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/response_regeneration/)
- Base-model generation throughput anchors for Qwen3-8B, single stream: 151.81 tok/s on H20 (vLLM), 187 tok/s on H200 (SGLang). — [AngelSlim card](https://huggingface.co/AngelSlim/Qwen3-8B_eagle3); [Tengyunw card](https://huggingface.co/Tengyunw/qwen3_8b_eagle3)
- Historical/qualitative anchors: original EAGLE (v1) is "trainable (within 1-2 days) on 8x RTX 3090"; the Vertex team's offline pipeline (70B-class target) took "about one day on a single host" on the initial dataset and "several days" with the larger one, GPU type unstated. — [SafeAILab/EAGLE README](https://github.com/SafeAILab/EAGLE); [LMSYS blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2025-12-01-eagle3-vertex/)
- A cloud-vendor blog claims a custom EAGLE-3 head takes "2-4 hours on 4x H100s" without naming model size or dataset (snippet only; weak source). — [Spheron blog](https://www.spheron.network/blog/eagle-3-speculative-decoding-gpu-cloud/)
- Small-data floor: 2,100 conversations x 5 epochs on 1x H100 yields acceptance length only ~1.45 on English versus 2.37 for a fully trained head; 5K-sample sanity runs reach ~1.5. — [SwitchXDDD card](https://huggingface.co/SwitchXDDD/multilingual-eagle3-qwen3-8b); [Train a Speculator tutorial](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/user_guide/tutorials/train/)

### Inferences
(All arithmetic below is mine, derived from the cited inputs; none is a published end-to-end figure.)
- Online training (hidden-state forward + head update together): at 126,640 tok/s on 8x H200, 1B token-epochs takes about 2.2 h wall-clock, about 17.5 H200-hours. At the un-optimised baseline (63,015 tok/s) it is about 35 H200-hours. Examples: Tengyunw-scale 1B tokens x 2 epochs is roughly 35 H200-hours with SpecForge; SpecBundle-scale 1.4M conversations x 2 epochs would be on the same order to a few times more depending on tokens per conversation (token count not published). Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B have the same hidden size (4096), so the figure should transfer approximately. H100 would be somewhat slower; no measured H100/A100 training throughput was found.
- WhiFlash recipe: 800K steps x batch 8 = 6.4M sample-passes over 464K examples, about 13.8 epochs. If that is ~0.8-1B tokens per epoch, it is roughly 11-14B token-epochs, i.e. on the order of 200-250 H200-hours at SpecForge throughput. This is the expensive "follow the paper closely" end; SpecBundle's 2-epoch recipe is the cheap end.
- Response regeneration (step a when responses must be on-policy) is likely the larger hidden cost: the documented 1K samples per 10 min on 2x H100 extrapolates to about 77 h (about 155 H100-hours) for 464K samples, but that figure includes server startup and a small-sample run, so treat it as a loose upper bound; a batched vLLM server at high concurrency should do materially better. Forwarding existing text for hidden states (no generation) is prefill-only and is already included in the online training throughput above.
- Offline cache size for Qwen3-8B (d=4096, bf16): 8,192 bytes per token per captured layer. Last-layer-only (MTP-style head): about 8.2 TB per 1B tokens. EAGLE-3 with 3 layers: about 24.6 TB per 1B tokens; with 4 tensors as in the speculators example: about 32.8 TB per 1B tokens. This excludes token embeddings (recomputable from ids). Online mode needs none of it.
- Training only the head on cached states would be cheaper than the online numbers above (no 8B forward), but no source isolates head-only training throughput for a 0.2-0.4B head; the TTT unrolling (length 7) multiplies head compute roughly 7x per token.
- Quality-versus-data: evidence points to needing at least several hundred thousand regenerated conversations (roughly 0.5-1B tokens) to reach acceptance length ~2+ at chain depth 2-3; thousands of samples give ~1.5.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end GPU-hour or wall-clock figure for training an EAGLE-3 or MTP head on any 7-8B base on H100/A100 was found (EAGLE-3 paper, SpecForge README, speculators tutorial, model cards all omit it). WhiFlash and Tengyunw do not state training time.
- No measured batched prefill or generation throughput for Qwen3-8B at high concurrency on H100 was found in the sources opened; only single-stream numbers.
- No published token counts for Open-PerfectBlend or for the RedHatAI Qwen3-8B training set.
- No source reports head-only (offline) training throughput separately from target forward cost.
